Understanding Environmental Indexes

To provide a healthy outdoor experience, this tool pulls localized meteorological data based on your exact GPS coordinates rather than generic city-level forecasts. Knowing these metrics helps you plan walks, sports, or decide when to apply sunscreen.

How UV Index Works

The Ultraviolet (UV) Index predicts the intensity of UV radiation from the sun. A rating of 0-2 (Low) means you can safely stay outside, while an index of 8-10 (Very High) means unprotected skin can burn quickly. At high levels, sunglasses and SPF 30+ sunscreen are strictly recommended.

European Air Quality Index (EAQI)

We use the European Air Quality standard which factors in five key pollutants: Particulate Matter (PM10 and PM2.5), Ozone (O3), Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2), and Sulphur Dioxide (SO2). Lower numbers indicate cleaner, healthier air for outdoor activities.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where does this environmental data come from?

Once your device determines your GPS coordinates, the tool communicates with open-source meteorological APIs (like Open-Meteo) which aggregate data from national weather services and environmental satellites worldwide.

Why does the Air Quality Index (AQI) change throughout the day?

Air quality fluctuates based on traffic rush hours, industrial activity, and weather patterns. For instance, wind can blow smog away, while temperature inversions can trap pollution near the ground level.
