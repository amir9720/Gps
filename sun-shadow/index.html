How Sun Altitude and Shadow Length Works

The position of the sun in the sky is primarily determined by two angles: Altitude and Azimuth. Using complex celestial mechanics formulas running securely inside your browser, this tool calculates the exact geometry of light rays hitting your location.

Altitude (Elevation Angle)

The altitude angle tells you how high the sun is above the horizon. 0° means the sun is exactly on the horizon (sunrise or sunset), and 90° means the sun is perfectly straight overhead (zenith). When the altitude is negative, the sun has set, and there are no shadows.

Azimuth (Direction)

The azimuth tells you the compass direction of the sun. It is measured in degrees clockwise from True North. For example, an azimuth of 90° means the sun is due East, 180° is South, and 270° is West.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the shadow length calculated?

We use basic right-angle trigonometry. The length of the shadow (L) equals the height of the object (h) divided by the tangent of the sun's altitude angle (L = h / tan(altitude)). The lower the sun gets in the sky, the longer the shadow stretches.

Why is this useful for indoor plants or apartments?

Real estate buyers and plant enthusiasts use this tool to determine how far direct sunlight will penetrate into a room through a window. By treating the window frame as the "object height", you can calculate exactly where the shadow ends and the sunny patch begins on your floor.
