The "15-Minute City" Concept Explained

The 15-minute city is an urban planning concept proposing that most daily necessities and services—such as work, shopping, education, healthcare, and leisure—should be easily reachable by a 15-minute walk or bike ride from any point in the city. This tool helps you visualize exactly what falls within that boundary from your doorstep.

Average Walking Speeds

The radius calculations in this tool are based on the globally accepted average human walking speed of roughly 3 miles per hour (4.8 km/h or 80 meters per minute). A 15-minute walk therefore correlates to a straight-line radius of approximately 1,200 meters (1.2 km).

Real Estate & Tourism Benefits

Whether you are evaluating a new apartment to rent or choosing a hotel for your vacation, visualizing the walkable radius helps assess neighborhood convenience. High walkability scores correlate with healthier lifestyles, reduced transportation costs, and lower carbon footprints.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the circle account for streets and buildings?

No, the circle represents "as the crow flies" (straight-line) distance. Actual walking distance will be slightly longer depending on the layout of streets, pedestrian crossings, and natural barriers like rivers or highways.

Can I use this tool anywhere in the world?

Yes! The underlying OpenStreetMap data has global coverage. You can use your device's GPS to find your current location in any country, or manually tap on the map to inspect neighborhoods in other cities before traveling.
