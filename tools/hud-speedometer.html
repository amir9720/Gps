How GPS HUD Speedometer Works

A Head-Up Display (HUD) projects visual metrics directly into your line of sight. By placing your smartphone on your vehicle dashboard facing upwards at night, this tool mirrors the speed readout horizontally. The reflection on your windshield glass appears right-side-up, allowing you to monitor driving velocity without shifting focus from the road.

GPS Speed vs. Car Odometer

Traditional vehicle odometers calculate speed based on wheel tire rotations, which can introduce a 3% to 7% variance caused by tire wear, rim size changes, or tire pressure. In contrast, GPS speed is computed by measuring Doppler frequency shifts and real-time positional coordinate intervals from satellites, offering true ground speed under clear sky conditions.

Optimizing HUD Reflection

For the clearest windshield reflection without ghosting, set your phone screen brightness to 100%, activate HUD Mirror Mode, and place a dark anti-slip mat on your dashboard beneath the device. This minimizes secondary internal glass glare during nighttime journeys.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the speedometer show 0 or fluctuate when standing still?

Civilian satellite receivers experience slight atmospheric signal delays (GPS jitter) when stationary. This tool incorporates a dead-band filter that zeroes out movements under 1.5 km/h to prevent drifting while stopped at traffic lights.

Does HUD mode require an internet connection while driving?

No. Once this webpage finishes loading in your mobile browser, all speed calculations and screen mirror rendering execute locally via the hardware GPS receiver chip without consuming continuous mobile data.

Will the phone screen turn off automatically?

When tracking is active, this tool utilizes the modern Screen Wake Lock API to prevent your phone from entering sleep mode, keeping the HUD reflection visible throughout your trip.
