Understanding Your GPS Sensor Health

Your smartphone relies on an internal A-GPS (Assisted GPS) chip that communicates with orbital satellites (like GPS, GLONASS, or Galileo) alongside cellular tower triangulation. The "Error Margin" displayed above indicates the radius of uncertainty around your reported location.

Accuracy Benchmark Tiers

  • Excellent (0-5 meters): Optimal satellite lock. Usually achieved outdoors under a clear sky.
  • Good (6-15 meters): Average urban performance. Slight interference from trees or low buildings.
  • Poor (15+ meters): Weak signal. Common indoors, in basements, or dense urban canyons with tall skyscrapers.

How to Calibrate Your Sensor

If your accuracy remains "Poor" even when standing outdoors, your device's internal compass and GPS cache might be out of sync. To fix this, open your maps application and move your phone in a "Figure 8" motion several times. This resets the magnetometer and forces a fresh satellite handshake.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my altitude reading blank or inaccurate?

Altitude (elevation above sea level) requires a 3D satellite lock connecting to at least four distinct satellites. Some older smartphones or budget devices do not pass altitude data to the browser, resulting in a blank reading. Even when available, consumer GPS altitude is typically less accurate than horizontal latitude/longitude.

Can Wi-Fi improve my GPS accuracy?

Yes. Modern devices use Wi-Fi Positioning Systems (WPS) alongside traditional GPS. By keeping your Wi-Fi turned on (even if you are not connected to a network), your phone scans nearby routers to instantly pinpoint your location indoors where satellite signals fail to penetrate.
